# Living the day

This guide covers what **Now**, **Day** and **History** show and how to use them.

## Now

Now is the main working screen: the date, the plan's status and your situation, then one card with the thing to do now (an action, the running session, or why nothing fits) and how much of today is still free. **Up next** sits beside or below it. A new install offers **Start setup** instead.

A word beside the clock says whether the page is still true: **Plan current**, **Plan not current**, **Saving...**, **Updating plan...**, **Couldn't update plan**, or **Refreshing...** when a newer plan exists. A small dot means this tab hears about changes as they happen. If the wait runs long, Now asks **Is the planner worker running?**

Your **situation** (place, what you carry, energy) opens everything the engine knows about you:

- **Current energy**: High, Medium, Low or Recovery.
- **Where you are**: a saved place; Now asks again once the answer is old.
- **With you**: the portable capabilities you carry.
- **How you can get about**: car, bike or on foot; this decides whether work elsewhere is offered as a trip. **Travel off** stops trips for errands.

Changing any of these recalculates the recommendation immediately.

### When an action is recommended

The card says **Do this now**, for how long and until when, then the action and its project. **Why this, now** gives the reason, whether it fits before your next commitment, and how its area is doing. **How this was worked out** shows the focused minutes, the evidence and the runner-up.

- **Start** creates a recorded work session. Use **Started earlier?** to backdate it (10 or 30 minutes, or a time you pick).
- **Not now** opens reasons it cannot be worked on; one tap marks it blocked. Reasons: missing information, waiting on someone, dependency, tool or condition unavailable, energy or health, technical problem, other.
- **Something else...** offers up to three alternatives that fit, with optional reasons for switching: better fit, lower energy, condition unavailable, deadline judgment, maintain momentum, personal priority, other.

These reasons are recorded as evidence. Choosing an alternative never silently changes the planner's ranking.

### When the next thing is a journey

Work at another place is not impossible; it is a journey. Now then shows the leg ("Drive to the office, 18 min") and what waits at the other end, or **Leave by 08:10** when the plan requires it, with the usual and the cautious duration. **I'm at ...** completes the leg: it is the same location assertion as the situation panel, so the plan re-ranks from where you now are. Once your place is stale, Now says it is **assuming** a place until you confirm it. **Measure travel** spends one live travel-time lookup when the figure is not already live.

When work elsewhere is not the lead, Now lists **Worth the trip** (the journey was costed and would be worth taking) and **Not worth it right now** (blocked only by being elsewhere, with the reason).

### During a session

- **Done** stops the segment and opens feedback. For a routine or commitment it records it finished.
- **Pause** closes the current segment; paused time is not work. **Resume** opens a new one.
- **Something else** offers rarer endings: **Blocked**, **Skip**, **Did not start**.

Refreshing the page reconstructs a running or paused session from the database. At the checkpoint, **How did it go?** asks for one answer:

- **Finished it**: completes the action. On a cadence it reads **Done for today** and closes that cycle only.
- **Made progress**: records the session and sets the remaining effort you pick.
- **Not sure**: keeps the previous estimate and flags it for review.
- **Keep going**: appears only when another segment would not displace more important work.
- **Got stuck**: pick a reason; the action moves to waiting or blocked.
- **Didn't start**: records the outcome without reducing remaining effort.

### When nothing fits

Now names what would resolve it where it can:

- **One answer would open up work**: the only obstacle is a question nobody has answered, such as where you are or whether you have a capability with you. Answer it on the spot.
- **Nothing fits before ...**: the work is possible but the moment is not. **Work later tonight** moves the day's horizon out.

Otherwise expand **Why nothing fits** to see the calculation evidence: too little time in the slot, no satisfied requirement pathway, insufficient energy, an incomplete dependency, or an action that is not yet available.

### Routine steps, preparation and check-ins

- Right after you say you are awake, Now leads with the wake routine's current step.
- When preparation for the next commitment no longer fits, a banner says **Behind prep pace** (or **Prep is tight**). A step that must start by a certain time gets its own warning.
- A flexible preparation step that is ready appears under **Can do now**.
- Now periodically asks **Still on ...?** (roughly every 15 to 20 minutes, never during sleep). **On track** and **Something else** answer it. A missed slot is asked once in the past tense. After about half an hour of silence the plan counts you away and **Welcome back** helps you record what happened.
- While a confirmed commitment (or its setup, travel or transition) holds the clock, Now shows that block instead of a recommendation. Start only records that the time really went there.

### When something comes up

**Something came up** on Now, and the **Disruption** half of the **+** sheet, write an unplanned event straight into the day as a confirmed commitment and replan around it at once. There is no Inbox step. You are asked a few coarse questions (what, how long, whether you are free in the meantime) because this is filled in on the move.

## Day

**Day** shows one day at a time, with **Week** beside it. A sentence under the date says how much time is free and what the day is built around.

**Day plan** draws the day to scale from wake to bed: commitments with their setup and transition, scheduled preparation, routines, recurring windows, recorded sessions, the running session and the current directive. Only the first block is firm; later blocks are a projection of the engine following its own recommendation, drawn lighter and revised as the day changes. A commitment you travel to shows when to leave. A strip beside the hours shows where the projected day puts you.

A card beside the plan gives today's free time, time recorded so far, and the sleep tonight's bedtime leaves. Rows open sheets:

- **Direction for today**: choose up to three **desired wins** and defer an action with a reason. Intent never bypasses feasibility.
- **Change the day**: add a commitment and see what a disruption changed.
- **Sleep plan**, **Recorded today**.

**Sleep is a wall.** The engine never shortens a night for work. It places each night for the most sleep it can give, as close to your preferred times as possible, routing around commitments. When the fullest night would start much earlier than usual, it warns and offers a nearer alternative. The **Sleep** page lists the last thirty nights, where you can correct a tracked night or log one the app never saw.

**Week** shows seven days on one set of hours, so a weekly class reads as a pattern, with each day's free time and any deliverable due.

### Fixed commitments

A fixed commitment removes time from capacity. Enter its title, start and end, place, setup time before and transition time after, and status:

- **Confirmed**: occupied time.
- **Tentative**: kept as a provisional "maybe".
- **Cancelled**: no longer blocks the plan.

The **place** is one of your saved places (naming it lets the planner work out the journey). You can also set the project the time belongs to, whether you are **fully occupied** or **free to do other things** while there, and how often check-ins interrupt. A commitment can **repeat**: every N days, every N weeks on chosen weekdays, or monthly. A single date in a series can be edited or cancelled without touching the rest.

## History

History shows immutable work-session evidence. It is not a score or a streak. Each session is a card: its title, source and result, and its numbers: planned and active minutes, paused and feedback minutes, remaining effort before and after, any blocking or alternative reason, and your note. Protected sleep and routine steps appear as their own kinds of session. Use History to check whether estimates were realistic and whether pauses and overruns were recorded correctly. A labelled span of days (a trip, days off sick) can be marked on the calendar; it is display only and never changes the plan.
